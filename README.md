# where to buy arbitrum: Top Exchanges Compared, Fees, and How to Actually Get ARB

If you're searching "where to buy arbitrum," you're almost certainly past the "what is Arbitrum" stage and into the "where do I actually click buy" stage. ARB is the governance token of the Arbitrum ecosystem — a Layer-2 network that batches Ethereum transactions off-chain using Optimistic Rollups to cut gas costs and speed up confirmation. It's listed on most major centralized exchanges, so the question isn't really *if* you can buy it, but *which* platform makes the most sense given your location, payment method, and how much you care about fees.

This guide walks through the realistic options, what each one costs you in fees and friction, and how to actually execute the trade once you've picked a venue. I'll lean on OKX as a worked example because it consistently ranks as one of the highest-volume ARB spot venues, but the same logic applies if you end up on Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, or Bybit.

## Step-by-step: buying ARB with USDT on spot

If you've decided on a centralized exchange (OKX used here as the example, but the steps are nearly identical on Binance, Bybit, etc.):

1. **Fund your account with USDT.** Either deposit USDT directly from another wallet (free on OKX, ~$2–5 in network gas from your end), or buy USDT with fiat via bank transfer or card.
2. **Open the ARB/USDT spot pair.** On OKX: Trade → Spot → search "ARB".
3. **Choose order type.**
   - **Market** — fills immediately at the best available price. You pay the taker fee (0.10%). Use this if you just want in now.
   - **Limit** — you set the price. If it fills passively, you pay the maker fee (0.08%). Slightly cheaper, but you wait.
4. **Enter amount.** You can specify in ARB or in USDT. Double-check the units — ARB is currently trading under $0.20, so "100" means 100 ARB (~$10–15), not $100 worth.
5. **Confirm.** The order executes (market) or rests on the book (limit).
6. **Decide on custody.** Leaving ARB on the exchange is fine for short-term trading. For longer holds, withdraw to a self-custody wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, or a hardware wallet connected to Arbitrum One).

## Buying ARB without an exchange: the DEX route

If you already have ETH on Arbitrum One or USDC on Arbitrum, you can skip centralized venues entirely:

1. Connect a wallet (MetaMask, Rabby, OKX Web3 wallet) to Arbitrum One.
2. Go to Uniswap, SushiSwap, or 1inch.
3. Swap ETH or USDC → ARB.

There's no KYC, no account, and no withdrawal fee — but you pay Arbitrum gas (typically sub-cent per swap) plus the DEX's swap fee (0.05%–0.3% depending on the pool) plus slippage. For small-to-medium purchases this is often cheaper than a centralized exchange once you net out card fees and KYC friction. For larger trades, the slippage on a relatively thin liquidity token like ARB can exceed the 0.10% you'd pay on OKX or Binance.

## Things people get wrong on the first ARB purchase

A handful of recurring mistakes worth flagging:

- **Buying on the wrong network.** ARB exists on Arbitrum One, not Ethereum mainnet. If you withdraw ARB from an exchange, select the Arbitrum One network. Sending to an Ethereum mainnet ARB address (which doesn't really exist as a separate token) or to a wrong-network wallet can mean lost funds.
- **Confusing price units.** ARB trades around $0.08–$0.12 recently. "Buying 50" means 50 ARB (~$5), not $50 worth. Always check whether the amount field is denominated in ARB or in quote currency.
- **Using Convert for large trades.** OKX Convert is marketed as zero-fee, but the spread is the fee. For anything over a few hundred dollars, spot market with a limit order is usually cheaper.
- **Ignoring the card markup.** Card purchases are fast but expensive. If time isn't critical, a bank transfer + spot trade saves 2–3%.
- **Assuming US availability.** OKX, Bybit, and Binance's global platforms are not available to US users. If you're US-based, your realistic options are Coinbase, Kraken, and Binance.US.

## Storing ARB after you buy

ARB is an ERC-20 token on the Arbitrum One network. Storage options:

- **On the exchange** — fine for trading, but you don't control the keys. Not recommended for long-term holds or large amounts.
- **Software wallet on Arbitrum One** — MetaMask, Rabby, Trust Wallet, OKX Web3 wallet. Free, you control the keys, but you're responsible for seed phrase security.
- **Hardware wallet** — Ledger and Trezor both support Arbitrum One via MetaMask or Rabby bridge. Best option for meaningful holdings.

Whichever you pick, make sure the receiving address is on the **Arbitrum One** network, not Ethereum mainnet.

## A note on ARB itself

ARB is a governance token — it votes on Arbitrum DAO proposals, it doesn't pay dividends or staking yield natively. The tokenomics are 10 billion total supply with a circulating portion that releases over time via DAO-controlled vesting. Price action has been rough: ARB hit an all-time high of $2.405 in January 2024 and has since traded down to the $0.08–$0.12 range — roughly a 95% drawdown from peak. That's not unusual for L2 governance tokens in a quiet market, but it's worth being clear-eyed about: you're buying a governance asset on a Layer-2 network, not a yield-bearing instrument.

If your thesis is "I want exposure to Arbitrum's ecosystem growth," ARB is the most direct way to express it. If your thesis is "I want to use Arbitrum cheaply," you don't actually need to buy ARB — you just need ETH on Arbitrum One to pay gas.

## Quick FAQ

**Can I buy ARB with a credit card directly?**
On most exchanges, not in one step. You typically buy USDT or USDC with the card, then trade that for ARB in spot. Some wallet apps (OKX Web3, Trust Wallet with integrated ramps) let you buy ARB "directly" with a card, but the same two-step swap happens behind the scenes — and the card markup still applies.

**Is ARB available in the US?**
Yes, on Coinbase and Kraken. Binance.US lists it in some states. OKX, Bybit, and Binance's global platforms are not available to US residents.

**What's the cheapest way to buy ARB?**
Bank transfer to an exchange → swap to USDT → limit order on ARB/USDT spot. That gets you to roughly 0.08% in trading fees plus whatever the bank charges (often nothing). Card purchases add 2–3% on top.

**Do I need to complete KYC?**
On any regulated centralized exchange, yes — both to deposit fiat and to trade in most regions. DEXes don't require KYC but require you to already hold crypto to swap with.

**Should I leave ARB on the exchange?**
For active trading, sure. For a long-term hold, withdraw to a self-custody wallet on Arbitrum One. Exchange risk is real — FTX users learned this the hard way.
